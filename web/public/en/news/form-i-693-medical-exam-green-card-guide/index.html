Form I-693 medical exam for a green card: who does it, when, and what to bring

Simple guide to the I-693 immigration medical exam: the authorized civil surgeon, filing it with Form I-485, vaccines, cost, and common mistakes.

If you apply for a green card inside the U.S. with Form I-485, you almost always need Form I-693, Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record.

This form shows USCIS that you meet the health requirements to become a permanent resident.

Who does the exam

  • Only a USCIS-designated civil surgeon can perform the exam and sign Form I-693.
  • Your regular doctor will not work unless USCIS has designated them.
  • Find a civil surgeon near you with the official USCIS "Find a Doctor" tool.

When to file it

  • USCIS requires Form I-693 to be submitted together with your Form I-485. This rule applies to applications filed on or after December 2, 2024.
  • If the I-693 is missing, USCIS may reject your I-485 and send the package back.
  • The civil surgeon's signature must be dated no more than 60 days before you file your I-485. Plan the exam with that in mind.

What to bring

  • A valid photo ID (for example, a passport).
  • Your vaccination record, if you have one.
  • A list of medicines you take and papers about major medical treatments.
  • Payment for the doctor. USCIS charges no fee for Form I-693, but the civil surgeon charges for the exam and prices vary by office.

What they check

  • A general physical exam and questions about your health history.
  • Tests required by CDC rules, such as tuberculosis screening and some blood tests depending on your age.
  • Your vaccines. If you are missing any, the doctor can give them or tell you where to get them.

Common mistakes

  • Doing the exam too early, so the signature is older than 60 days when you file.
  • Opening or changing the form the doctor gives you. Follow their exact instructions on how to submit it.
  • Choosing a doctor who is not designated by USCIS.

What to do

  • Find your civil surgeon with the official USCIS tool and book the appointment shortly before you file your I-485.
  • Check that your name and date of birth are correct on the form.
  • Keep a copy for yourself if the doctor gives you one.
  • Turn on CaseAlerta alerts so you know as soon as your I-485 status changes.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your specific case, talk to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative.
