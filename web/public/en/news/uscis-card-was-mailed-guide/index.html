"Card Was Mailed To Me": USCIS sent your green card or work permit. How to track it and what to do if it doesn't arrive

What the "Card Was Mailed To Me" status means, how to track your green card or work permit with USPS, and what to do if it doesn't arrive.

When your case changes to "Card Was Mailed To Me," USCIS has printed your card and handed it to the U.S. Postal Service (USPS). It may be your green card, your work permit (EAD), or another document.

Before that you usually see "Card Is Being Produced." That is good news: your case was approved.

Where it goes

  • USCIS mails the card by USPS to the address on your case.
  • If your attorney asked for it, it can go to your attorney's or representative's office.
  • USCIS generally mails only to U.S. addresses.

How to track it

  • Create a USCIS Case Status Online account. It shows your USPS tracking number.
  • Use that number to follow the package on the USPS website.
  • Sign up for USPS Informed Delivery to preview your mail before it arrives.
  • Delivery can take several days after the notice.

If you moved

  • You must tell USCIS your new address within 10 days of moving.
  • Update it online with USCIS and with USPS, for every pending case.
  • Write the full address in USPS format (use the USPS ZIP Code lookup).

If it doesn't arrive

  • Check USPS tracking and ask at your local post office.
  • If it still can't be found, use the USCIS "Non-Delivery of Card" e-Request. Don't send it if you were just approved: give the mail time.
  • If the card is truly lost, you may need to request a replacement (I-90 for a green card, I-765 for a work permit).

What to do

  • Confirm today that your address with USCIS is correct.
  • Find your tracking number and turn on Informed Delivery.
  • When it arrives, check your name, date of birth, and card dates. If USCIS made an error, ask for a correction.
  • Turn on CaseAlerta alerts to know the moment your case changes.

This is general information, not legal advice.
