How to ask USCIS to expedite your case: a simple guide

What a USCIS expedite request is, which reasons USCIS accepts, how to ask, what evidence to send, and what to do if your case is just slow.

An expedite request is when you ask USCIS to review your case ahead of its normal turn.

USCIS decides case by case. Asking is free, but approval is not guaranteed.

First: is your case overdue or urgent?

  • If your case is just slow, check the official processing times page. If you are past the date USCIS shows for inquiries, you can submit a "case outside normal processing time" inquiry through e-Request.
  • An expedite request is different: it is for a real emergency that cannot wait.

Reasons USCIS considers

  • Severe financial loss to a company or a person.
  • Urgent humanitarian reasons (for example, a serious illness).
  • Nonprofit organizations furthering U.S. cultural or social interests.
  • U.S. government interests (for example, a request from a federal agency).
  • A clear USCIS error.

How to ask

  • Call the USCIS Contact Center or use your USCIS online account to request it.
  • Have your receipt number ready (it is on your I-797 notice).
  • Briefly explain why it is urgent and what deadline you face.
  • If USCIS asks for evidence, send it quickly: medical letters, employer letters, flight tickets, official notices.

Good to know

  • If your form is eligible for premium processing, USCIS generally expects you to use that option instead of an expedite request.
  • An approved expedite does not change the requirements: the case is decided under the same rules.
  • You can ask for an expedite even if you already sent an outside-normal-processing-time inquiry.

What to do

  • 1. Check the processing times for your form and office.
  • 2. If it is just slow, use e-Request when USCIS allows it.
  • 3. If there is a real emergency, gather evidence and request an expedite.
  • 4. Turn on CaseAlerta alerts so you know right away when your case changes.
