Employment green card (PERM): what the Instacart and Cloudera settlements mean

The government settled with Instacart and Cloudera over PERM recruiting. Here is what PERM is and what to do if your employer sponsors you.

In October 2026, the Department of Justice announced two settlements with companies over how they recruited workers in the PERM process.

PERM is the Department of Labor's labor certification. It is the first step of many employment-based green cards. Before sponsoring a worker, the company must make a good-faith effort to find U.S. workers for the job.

What happened

  • Instacart (October 19): settlement with the Justice Department's Immigrant and Employee Rights Section (IER) and the EEOC. According to the government, it required mailed applications for PERM jobs, while it accepted electronic applications for its other jobs.
  • Instacart will pay up to $4 million into a fund for affected people and $1 million in civil penalties. It will have 3 years of training and monitoring.
  • Cloudera (October 13): settlement with IER and the Department of Labor. According to the government, it used a non-working email address to receive applications for PERM jobs.
  • Cloudera will pay $600,000 in civil penalties and up to $1.4 million into a back-pay fund. It will also have 3 years of training and monitoring, and the settlement resolves the suspension of its PERM certifications.
  • Instacart said its practices complied with the law and that it settled to stay focused on its business.

What it means for you

  • These settlements are about how companies recruit. They do not change green card rules or USCIS forms.
  • The agencies expect PERM recruiting to use the same channels and methods the company uses for its other jobs.
  • If your employer sponsors you, recruiting that follows these practices makes your process stronger.

What to do

  • If your company sponsors you, ask its immigration attorney whether its PERM recruiting follows normal hiring practices.
  • If you already have an I-140 or I-485 pending, track it with your receipt number. CaseAlerta alerts you when it changes.
  • Check the Visa Bulletin each month to see if your priority date is current.
  • If you are an employer, make sure your PERM ads accept applications the same way as your other jobs.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your case, talk to an immigration attorney.
