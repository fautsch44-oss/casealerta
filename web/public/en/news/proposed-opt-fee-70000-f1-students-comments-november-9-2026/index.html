OPT for F-1 students: DHS proposes a $70,000 fee (not in effect yet; comments due November 9)

DHS proposes schools pay $70,000 for each F-1 student's first OPT and $30,000 for later ones. It is only a proposal. What to do.

On October 8, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), through ICE, published a proposed rule in the Federal Register called "Optional Practical Training Fees."

OPT (Optional Practical Training) lets F-1 students work in their field of study.

What it proposes, in plain words

  • $70,000 per student the first time the school recommends OPT (before or after graduation).
  • $30,000 for each later OPT recommendation, including the STEM OPT extension.
  • The SEVP-certified school pays. The proposal does not stop the school from passing the cost to the student or employer.
  • Payment would be due before the DSO (school official) enters the recommendation in SEVIS. Without payment, USCIS would not grant work authorization.

Most important: it does not apply yet

  • This is a proposal. It is not a final rule.
  • Per the text, the fee would apply only to recommendations dated on or after the final rule's effective date, proposed as 60 days after the final rule is published.
  • You do not have to pay anything new for your OPT today.

Key dates

  • Public comments: through November 9, 2026, at regulations.gov, docket ICEB-2026-0100.
  • Comments on the information collection (Paperwork Reduction Act): through December 7, 2026.
  • DHS will not consider comments sent by email, letter or hand delivery. Use the portal only.

What to do

  • Do not cancel or rush your OPT application out of fear. Talk to your school's DSO first.
  • If you want to weigh in, comment at regulations.gov using ICEB-2026-0100 before November 9, 2026.
  • If you already filed your Form I-765 for OPT, track it with your receipt number. CaseAlerta alerts you when it changes.
  • Watch the Federal Register for a final rule and its effective date.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your case, talk to an immigration attorney or your school's DSO.
