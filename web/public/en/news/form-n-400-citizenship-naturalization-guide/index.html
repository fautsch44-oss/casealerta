Form N-400 (citizenship): requirements, when to apply and the steps

A simple guide to Form N-400 to apply for U.S. citizenship: 5-year rules, filing 90 days early, the test, the interview and the oath.

Form N-400, Application for Naturalization, is the form you use to ask USCIS for U.S. citizenship.

It is filed by permanent residents (green card holders) who already meet the requirements. This guide explains the general 5-year rule.

General requirements (5-year rule)

  • Be at least 18 years old when you file the N-400.
  • Be a permanent resident for at least 5 years.
  • Continuous residence in the U.S. for those 5 years, right before you file.
  • Physical presence in the U.S. for at least 30 months within those 5 years.
  • Live at least 3 months in the state or USCIS district where you file.
  • Good moral character and attachment to the principles of the Constitution.
  • Read, write and speak basic English, and know U.S. history and government (civics test). Some older applicants with many years of residence have exceptions.

Other cases have different rules, for example spouses of U.S. citizens or military members. USCIS explains them on its citizenship pages.

You can file 90 days early

  • Under the 5-year rule, you can file the N-400 up to 90 days before you complete 5 years as a permanent resident.
  • Filing early does not make you eligible sooner. You still must complete the 5 years.

How to file

  • You can file online with a USCIS account or on paper. Use the current edition of the form.
  • Check the current fee in the USCIS Fee Schedule (G-1055) before you pay. Fees change.
  • Keep a copy of everything and write down your receipt number.

What happens next

  • You get a receipt notice (I-797C) with your case number.
  • USCIS schedules biometrics (fingerprints and photo), if needed.
  • You have an interview. The English and civics tests happen there.
  • USCIS decides: it approves, asks for more evidence or denies. If approved, it schedules your oath ceremony.
  • You become a citizen when you take the oath. You then receive your Certificate of Naturalization.
  • Timing varies by office. USCIS posts ranges on its processing times page. There is no exact decision date.

What to do

  • Check your green card date and find when you reach 5 years. Subtract 90 days: that is the first day you can file.
  • Count your trips outside the U.S. to confirm the 30 months of physical presence.
  • Read the official N-400 page and its instructions before you fill it out.
  • Study for the civics test with free USCIS materials.
  • When your receipt notice arrives, add the number in CaseAlerta to know when your status changes.
