"Case Was Rejected": why USCIS rejects an application and how to file again

Your USCIS status says Case Was Rejected. What it means, how it differs from a denial, common reasons, and how to file your application again.

If your USCIS online status says "Case Was Rejected," USCIS did not accept your application for processing.

It sounds serious, but it can usually be fixed. A rejection is not a decision on whether you qualify.

Rejection is not denial

  • Rejection: USCIS did not accept the package because of a form problem (signature, payment, form). Your case was not reviewed.
  • Denial: USCIS reviewed your case and decided you do not meet the requirements.
  • A rejected application does not keep its receipt date. When you file again, the new filing date counts.

Common reasons

  • The signature is missing or not valid.
  • The payment is missing, the amount is wrong, or the payment could not be processed.
  • You used an outdated edition of the form.
  • Required fields on the form are blank.
  • You mailed it to the wrong address or too early.

What happens to your payment

  • USCIS generally returns the package with a notice that explains the reason.
  • Read that notice carefully: it tells you what to fix.

What to do

  • Read the rejection notice and fix the exact problem it lists.
  • Download the current edition of the form on uscis.gov and check the current fee.
  • Sign by hand where required and double-check the payment method.
  • Mail it again to the correct address. If you have a deadline (for example, an extension), do it soon.
  • If you believe USCIS rejected it by mistake, contact the USCIS Contact Center.
  • Turn on CaseAlerta alerts to see your new filing's status right away.
