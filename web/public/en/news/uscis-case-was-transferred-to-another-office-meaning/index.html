"Case Was Transferred": what it means when USCIS moves your case to another office

Your USCIS status says your case was transferred. What it means, why it happens, whether your receipt number or timeline changes, and what to do next.

If your USCIS online status says "Case Was Transferred And A New Office Has Jurisdiction" (or a similar message), your case moved to another USCIS office.

It is an administrative step. It is not an approval or a denial.

Why USCIS transfers cases

  • To balance work across offices and move cases faster.
  • Because your case needs an interview or a step handled by a local field office.
  • Because you moved and updated your address with USCIS.

USCIS has announced many workload transfers between service centers and field offices. It is common.

What changes and what does not

  • Your receipt number does NOT change. Keep using it to check your case.
  • You usually get a notice in the mail (Form I-797C) naming the new office.
  • Processing time now depends on the new office. Check the official processing times page for that office.
  • Documents you already sent travel with your file. Do not resend them unless USCIS asks.

How long it takes after a transfer

There is no exact date. Each form and office has its own range. Check USCIS processing times for your form and the office shown on your notice.

Signs to watch for

  • A biometrics or interview appointment notice.
  • A Request for Evidence (RFE). If one arrives, answer before the deadline.
  • If you moved, confirm your address is current so you do not miss mail.

What to do

  • 1. Keep the transfer notice with your original receipt.
  • 2. Check processing times for the new office.
  • 3. Keep your address updated with USCIS (Form AR-11 or your online account).
  • 4. If your case goes past the new office's normal time, submit an e-Request inquiry.
  • 5. Turn on CaseAlerta alerts so you know right away when your case changes again.

This is general information, not legal advice. If you have questions about your case, talk to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative.
