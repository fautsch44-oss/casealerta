USCIS changes filing deadlines that fall on a weekend or holiday starting October 23, 2026

From October 23, only deadlines counted in days move to the next business day. Age or status-expiration cutoffs do not. What changes and what to do.

USCIS updated its Policy Manual to clarify what happens when a filing deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday. The alert is PA-2026-15, published September 23, 2026, and it applies starting October 23, 2026.

What still moves to the next business day

  • Deadlines counted in a number of days. For example, the 30 days to answer a Request for Evidence (RFE) or a Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID), or the deadline to file an appeal or motion.
  • If the last day falls on a weekend or holiday, USCIS treats a filing received by the end of the next business day as on time.

What NO LONGER moves to the next business day

  • Deadlines that are not counted in days but depend on a fact: your age, the date your status expires, or another eligibility requirement.
  • Example: if a child must file before turning 21 and the birthday is a Sunday, Monday is too late.
  • Example: if your I-94 expires on a Saturday, the extension must be filed before it expires. Do not count on Monday.
  • This reverses 2023 guidance that had moved these deadlines to the next business day.

If you file online

  • According to immigration attorneys' analysis, the next-business-day rule applies to paper filings. Online filings are treated as received when you submit them.

What to do

  • Check your dates: does your deadline depend on an age or on your status expiring? Then treat that day as the real limit and file earlier.
  • If you mail it, leave extra days: what counts is the date USCIS RECEIVES it, not the date you send it.
  • If you file online, do not wait until the last night.
  • If an important deadline is near a weekend, talk to an attorney or accredited representative.

This is general information, not legal advice.
