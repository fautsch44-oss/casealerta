USCIS biometrics (fingerprint) appointment: what to bring, what happens and how to reschedule

A simple guide to the USCIS fingerprint appointment: what to bring, what happens at the ASC and how to reschedule before the date.

After you file, USCIS may need your fingerprints, photo and signature. This is called a biometrics appointment. It happens at an Application Support Center (ASC).

The date, time and address are on a Form I-797C notice that USCIS mails you.

What to bring

  • Your appointment notice (I-797C). If you got more than one, bring them all.
  • A valid photo ID, such as a Green Card, passport or driver's license.
  • A printed or saved copy of the application you filed. USCIS cannot give you a copy at the appointment.
  • An interpreter if you don't understand spoken English, such as a family member. Your attorney does not need to come.

What happens at the appointment

  • Staff take your fingerprints, photo and, for some forms, a digital signature.
  • With that signature you confirm that what you filed was complete and true.
  • USCIS uses biometrics to confirm your identity and run background checks.
  • If you applied for a work permit (I-765) or Green Card replacement (I-90), they are also used to print your card.
  • Your name and personal details cannot be changed at the appointment.
  • Applicants 14 and older must sign. A parent or legal guardian may sign for a younger child.

How to reschedule

  • Call the USCIS Contact Center: 800-375-5283 (TTY 800-767-1833). Help is available in English and Spanish.
  • Call before the date and time of your original appointment.
  • You must show a good reason. If you are sick, follow these same steps.
  • If you miss it without rescheduling in time, USCIS may treat your case as abandoned and deny it.
  • If a medical condition keeps you at home, see the section for people with disabilities on your notice.

What to do

  • Check your I-797C today and write down the date, time and address.
  • Get your ID, the notice and your application copy ready the night before.
  • If you can't go, call USCIS before the appointment to reschedule.
  • If you moved, update your address with USCIS so you don't miss notices.
  • Add your receipt number in CaseAlerta to know when your status changes after the appointment.
  • To see how long the next step may take, check the official USCIS processing times. They are ranges, not exact dates.

This is general information, not legal advice.
