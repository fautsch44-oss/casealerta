"Case Was Received": what it means when USCIS receives your case and what comes next

Your USCIS status says Case Was Received. What it means, which receipt notice arrives, how to use your receipt date for processing times, and what to do next.

If your USCIS online status says "Case Was Received", USCIS accepted your form and opened a case with a receipt number.

This is the first step of the process. It does not mean approval or denial yet. It only confirms your case is in the system.

What arrives by mail

  • USCIS mails a receipt notice (Form I-797C, Notice of Action) to the address it has on file.
  • The notice shows your receipt number (13 characters, for example IOE, EAC or MSC) and your receipt date.
  • If you filed online, you will also see the notice in your USCIS online account.
  • Keep the notice. You will need it to check your status and for any inquiry.

Why the receipt date matters

  • USCIS measures processing times from the date it received your case.
  • On the official processing times page you pick your form and office, then enter your receipt date.
  • The tool tells you when you can contact USCIS if your case is taking longer than normal.
  • USCIS processing times are ranges based on recent cases and are updated monthly. They are not an exact decision date.

What usually happens next

  • Depending on the form, the next step may be a biometrics appointment (fingerprints and photo), a request for evidence (RFE), an interview, or a decision.
  • It is normal for the status to stay at "Case Was Received" for several weeks or months. That does not signal a problem.

What to do

  • Write down your receipt number and receipt date somewhere safe.
  • Check your status with your receipt number, or turn on CaseAlerta alerts to hear about every change right away.
  • If you move, update your address with USCIS (online or with Form AR-11). A USPS mail forwarding request does not update USCIS.
  • If your receipt notice never arrived, USCIS has an online request to report a notice you did not receive (e-Request).
  • Check the official processing times page before calling or submitting an inquiry.
