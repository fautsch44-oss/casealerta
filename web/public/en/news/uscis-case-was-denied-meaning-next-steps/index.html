"Case Was Denied": what it means when USCIS denies your case and your options

Your USCIS status says Case Was Denied. What it means, how to read the denial letter, deadlines to appeal or file a motion (I-290B), and what to do now.

If your USCIS online status says "Case Was Denied," USCIS reviewed your application and decided not to approve it.

It is hard news, but it is not always the end. There are often options, and time matters.

Denial is not rejection

  • Denial: USCIS did review your case and decided it does not meet the requirements, or that evidence was missing.
  • Rejection: USCIS did not accept the package because of a form problem, such as a signature or payment. Your case was not reviewed.

Read your denial letter

  • USCIS mails you a letter (decision) that explains the reasons.
  • The letter also says whether you can appeal or file a motion, with whom, and by when.
  • That deadline is short: generally 30 days from the decision (33 days if it was mailed to you). Check the exact date in your letter.

Your most common options

  • Motion to reopen: if you have new facts or evidence USCIS did not see. Filed on Form I-290B.
  • Motion to reconsider: if you believe USCIS misapplied the law or its policy to the evidence it already had. Also on Form I-290B.
  • Appeal to the Administrative Appeals Office (AAO): only for certain case types. Your letter says whether it applies. Also on Form I-290B.
  • File again: in some cases you can submit a new application with the missing evidence and the current fee.
  • Citizenship (N-400): if naturalization was denied, the path is to request a hearing on Form N-336, not Form I-290B.

Protect your status

  • A denial can affect your permission to stay or work in the U.S., especially if you relied on that application.
  • In some cases, USCIS may issue a Notice to Appear (NTA) before immigration court. If you get one, seek legal help right away.

What to do

  • Read the whole letter and write down the deadline it gives.
  • Gather what USCIS said was missing and check whether you have new evidence.
  • Talk soon with an immigration attorney or accredited representative: choosing between a motion, an appeal, or a new filing depends on your case.
  • Use the current edition of Form I-290B (or N-336) and the current fee posted on uscis.gov.
  • Turn on CaseAlerta alerts to see any change in your case or your motion right away.
