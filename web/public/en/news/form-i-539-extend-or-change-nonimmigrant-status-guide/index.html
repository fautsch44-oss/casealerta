Form I-539: how to extend your stay or change your nonimmigrant status in the U.S.

Simple guide to USCIS Form I-539: who uses it, when to file before your I-94 expires, who cannot use it, and what happens next.

Form I-539 lets you ask USCIS for more time in the United States, or to change from one temporary (nonimmigrant) status to another.

For example: a B-2 tourist who needs to stay longer, or a dependent family member of a worker or student.

First: check your I-94

  • Your deadline is not the visa date in your passport. It is the date on your I-94 record.
  • You can look up your I-94 on the CBP website (i94.cbp.dhs.gov).
  • USCIS recommends filing at least 45 days before your authorized stay expires.

Who CANNOT use Form I-539

  • People admitted under the Visa Waiver Program (ESTA).
  • People admitted in transit (for example, C-1) or as crewmembers (D).
  • Some people admitted as K fiancé(e)s or in other limited categories. Check the list on the official page.

How to file

  • In many cases you can file online with a USCIS account; in others, on paper.
  • Each extra family member included uses Form I-539A.
  • The fee depends on how you file. Always check the current fee at uscis.gov/g-1055 before paying.

What happens next

  • You get a receipt notice (I-797C) with your case number.
  • You may be scheduled for biometrics (fingerprints and photo).
  • USCIS may ask for more evidence (RFE).
  • Timing varies by office and category. There is no exact date: check the range on the USCIS processing times page.

What to do

  • Check your I-94 date today.
  • If you need more time, file Form I-539 before it expires, ideally with at least 45 days to spare.
  • Keep copies of everything, including your receipt.
  • Track your case with your receipt number (CaseAlerta alerts you when it changes).
  • If your stay already expired or you are unsure, talk to an immigration attorney. This is not legal advice.
