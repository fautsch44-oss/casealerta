Form I-751: how to remove conditions on your 2-year green card (marriage)

A simple I-751 guide: when to file (90 days before expiry), filing without your spouse, the 48-month extension, and what happens if you don't file.

If you got your green card through a marriage that was less than 2 years old, your residence is conditional. Your card lasts 2 years.

To keep permanent residence with a 10-year card, you must ask to remove the conditions with Form I-751 (Petition to Remove Conditions on Residence).

When to file

  • Within the 90 days before your conditional green card expires.
  • Example: if your card expires on June 1, your window opens about 90 days before that date.
  • Your children with conditional residence can be included on your petition if they got it at the same time or within 90 days after you.

Filing jointly with your spouse

The usual path is to file jointly with the same U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse you married, if you are still married.

Filing without your spouse (waiver)

You can ask for a waiver of the joint filing requirement if:

  • Your spouse died and the marriage was in good faith.
  • The marriage ended in divorce or annulment and was in good faith.
  • You or your child were battered or subjected to extreme cruelty by your spouse.
  • Your removal would cause extreme hardship.

You can request the waiver before or after the 90-day window. You can check more than one reason.

What happens if you don't file

  • Your conditional residence ends automatically.
  • USCIS notifies you and may start removal proceedings with a Notice to Appear (NTA).
  • If you file late, include a letter explaining why. USCIS decides whether you had good cause.

The 48-month extension

  • When you properly file the I-751, you get a receipt notice (Form I-797C).
  • That receipt extends your conditional residence and work authorization for 48 months.
  • Show the receipt together with your expired card as proof of status.
  • If your case is still pending after those 48 months, call the USCIS Contact Center (800-375-5283) to get evidence of status.

What comes next

  • You may be scheduled for biometrics (fingerprints and photo).
  • USCIS may call you for an interview. The notice arrives by mail with the date and place.
  • If approved, you get the 10-year green card.
  • If denied, USCIS explains in writing and may send your case to an immigration judge, who can review the decision.

Fee and timing

  • Fees change over time. Check the current amount on the official I-751 page or the USCIS fee calculator before you pay.
  • We don't give exact decision dates. Check the current range on the official USCIS processing times page.

What to do

  • Check the expiration date on your conditional green card today and mark the day your 90-day window opens.
  • Gather proof that the marriage is real: lease or mortgage, joint accounts, insurance, photos, children's birth certificates. The full list is in the I-751 instructions.
  • Keep the I-797C receipt in a safe place and carry it when you travel or start a job.
  • If you move, change your address with USCIS so you don't miss notices.
  • Add your receipt number to CaseAlerta to know the moment your status changes.
  • If you separated, there was abuse, or you have a record, talk to an immigration attorney before filing.
