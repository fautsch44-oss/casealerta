Form I-485 (adjustment of status): how to apply for a green card inside the U.S.

A simple guide to Form I-485: who files, when a visa is available, biometrics, the interview and what happens next. No made-up dates.

Form I-485 is the application to register permanent residence or adjust status. In plain words: it is how you apply for a green card without leaving the United States.

If you are outside the U.S., the usual path is consular processing, not the I-485.

Who files

  • People who are already in the U.S. and have an immigrant category (for example, family or employment).
  • Most people need a petition from a sponsor: Form I-130 (family) or I-140 (employment). Some categories do not.
  • A qualifying spouse and children can file their own I-485 as derivatives.

When you can file

  • In general, only when a visa is available in your category. That depends on your priority date and that month's Visa Bulletin.
  • Each month USCIS says which Visa Bulletin chart to use for adjustment of status.
  • Some categories let you file the I-485 together with the petition or while it is pending (concurrent filing). Others require the petition to be approved first.

How to file

  • Use the current edition listed on the official I-485 page. Old editions are rejected.
  • Sign the form. USCIS rejects unsigned forms.
  • Mail it to the address for your category (USCIS filing addresses page).
  • Check the current fee in the USCIS Fee Schedule (G-1055) or the fee calculator. The fee depends on your age. If you also ask for a work permit (I-765) or travel permit (I-131), check the fee for each one.
  • Some cases need supplements, like Supplement A (section 245(i)) or Supplement J (employment cases).

What happens next

  • You get a receipt notice (I-797C) with your case number.
  • USCIS schedules biometrics (fingerprints, photo and signature). Missing it without rescheduling can lead to a denial.
  • USCIS decides if you need an interview. If so, bring the originals of your documents, including your passport and I-94.
  • If USCIS asks for more evidence (RFE), answer before the deadline.
  • Decision: if approved, the card arrives by mail later. If denied, you may be able to file a motion to reopen or reconsider with Form I-290B.
  • If you move, tell USCIS within 10 days.
  • Timing varies by office and category. USCIS posts ranges on its processing times page. There is no exact decision date.

What to do

  • Confirm your category and check whether your priority date is current in this month's Visa Bulletin.
  • Read the official I-485 page and instructions before you fill it out.
  • Gather copies of your passport, I-94, birth certificate and the petition notice (I-130 or I-140).
  • Keep a copy of everything you send.
  • When your receipt notice arrives, add the number to CaseAlerta to know right away when your status changes.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your case, talk to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative.
