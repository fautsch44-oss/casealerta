Form I-90: how to renew or replace your green card (and the 36-month extension)

A simple I-90 guide: when to renew your green card, who should not use it, how to file, and how the receipt extends your card 36 months.

Form I-90 is the application to replace a Permanent Resident Card (green card). Use it to renew the card when it expires or to replace it if something is wrong.

Note: renewing the card is not renewing your residence. Your permanent residence does not expire; the card that proves it does.

When to use it

  • Your card expires within the next 6 months or has already expired.
  • It was lost, stolen or destroyed.
  • It was issued but you never received it.
  • It is damaged or has a data error.
  • You legally changed your name or other details.

Who should NOT use it

  • If you are a conditional resident (2-year card) and want to remove conditions, the I-90 is not your form. That uses a different form (I-751 or I-829, depending on your case).

How to file

  • Online, with a USCIS account, or by mail on paper.
  • If you request a fee waiver, you must file on paper.
  • Fees change over time. Check the current amount on the official I-90 page or the USCIS fee calculator before you pay.
  • If your card never arrived or has a USCIS error, in some cases there is no fee.

What happens next

  • You get a receipt notice (Form I-797C).
  • You may be scheduled for biometrics (fingerprints and photo).
  • USCIS reviews the case and mails your new card.

The 36-month extension

  • Since September 10, 2024, the I-90 receipt notice extends your green card's validity for 36 months from the expiration date printed on the card.
  • Show the receipt with your expired card as proof of status and work authorization.
  • If you no longer have the card and need proof of status, you can request a field office appointment through the USCIS Contact Center. They may give you an ADIT stamp.

How long it takes

We don't give exact dates. Check the current range on the official USCIS processing times page for Form I-90.

What to do

  • Check your card's date today. If it expires in 6 months or less, file the I-90.
  • Keep the I-797C receipt safe and carry a copy when traveling or starting a job.
  • If you move, update your address in your USCIS account so the card reaches you.
  • If your case is complex (criminal history, long trips abroad), talk to a lawyer before filing.
