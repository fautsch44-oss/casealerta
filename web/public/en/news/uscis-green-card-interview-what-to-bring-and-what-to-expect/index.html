USCIS green card interview (I-485): what to bring, what happens and what comes next

A simple guide to the adjustment of status (I-485) interview: original documents to bring, what they ask and what happens after.

When you apply for a green card from inside the U.S. with Form I-485, USCIS reviews your case and decides whether it needs to interview you.

If there is an interview, you get a notice with the date, time and office. In CaseAlerta you will see it as a status change, for example "Interview Was Scheduled".

What to bring

  • Your interview notice.
  • The originals of every document you sent with your I-485.
  • Your passports, travel documents and your I-94, even if they are expired.
  • A photo ID.
  • If your case is family-based, the relative who filed the petition should also attend, if it applies.
  • Check your notice: it may ask for extra documents.

What happens at the interview

  • You go to a USCIS office.
  • You answer questions under oath about your I-485 application.
  • The officer may ask for more evidence. A request for evidence comes with a deadline to respond.

What comes next

  • You usually do not get a written decision that day. USCIS mails you the decision.
  • If approved, you usually get an approval notice first and the green card later.
  • If denied, the notice explains why. In general you cannot appeal, but sometimes you can ask to reopen or reconsider with Form I-290B.

What to do

  • Read your interview notice today and write down the date, time and address.
  • Gather the originals of everything you sent, plus passports and I-94.
  • Review your application so you remember your answers.
  • If you moved, change your address with USCIS so you do not miss notices.
  • Add your receipt number to CaseAlerta to know the moment your status changes.
  • To see how long it may take, check USCIS processing times. They are ranges, not exact dates.

This is general information, not legal advice.
