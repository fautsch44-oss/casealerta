Citizenship (N-400): new USCIS memo on good moral character, public benefits, child support and sponsorship

On November 4, 2026, USCIS issued memo PM-602-0201 on good moral character for citizenship: public benefits, dependent support and sponsor duties.

On November 4, 2026, USCIS issued a policy memorandum (PM-602-0201) to its officers.

It covers how to decide whether a person has "good moral character" when applying for citizenship on Form N-400.

Good moral character is one of the requirements to become a citizen. The officer reviews it at your interview.

The three topics in the memo

  • Supporting dependents: willfully failing to support your children or other dependents can count against you.
  • Public benefits: getting benefits by illegal means, or intentionally avoiding self-sufficiency, can count against you.
  • Sponsorship: failing to meet a sponsor's obligations (for example, under Form I-864) can count against you.

What does not change

  • Lawfully receiving public benefits does not, by itself, stop you from becoming a citizen. That is how law firms that reviewed the memo explain it.
  • The officer must review your case individually and look at all your circumstances.
  • The dependent-support rule existed before the memo. If there were extenuating circumstances (for example, health or disability), you can explain them.

What is still unclear

  • Attorney analyses differ on how much weight an I-864 breach carries.
  • USCIS said it will update its Policy Manual and Form N-400. No date has been published yet.

What to do

  • If you plan to apply for citizenship, gather proof that you support your children (receipts, support orders, payments).
  • If you received public benefits, keep your approval letters and check that your applications were accurate.
  • If you sponsored someone with an I-864, keep proof of your support for that person.
  • Answer the N-400 truthfully. If you have doubts about your history, talk to an immigration attorney before filing.
